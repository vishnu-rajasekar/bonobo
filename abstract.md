**Title**
*Towards Equilibrium-Informed Sequential Growth for Structural Form Emergence*

**Abstract**
This research investigates how self-supporting structural form can emerge through sequential, equilibrium-informed growth rather than through post hoc analysis of already completed designs. The central claim is that structural generation should be organized as a layered process in which a canonical graph state, local grammar actions, evaluator-derived equilibrium signals, and benchmark-level acceptance logic remain explicit and separable. This framing is intended to keep the growth process interpretable, technically auditable, and compatible with later machine-learning support without collapsing structural semantics into opaque search machinery.

The immediate proof target is deliberately narrow: a benchmarked 2D Michell-style planar problem. The aim is not yet to solve all structural typologies or to claim a general learned generator, but to test whether materially efficient, support-seeking growth can be produced and inspected under a clear benchmark contract. In this setting, the project focuses on whether evaluator-derived signals can guide growth toward admissible intermediate states and improved equilibrium behavior, while preserving machine-readable traces of what rules were proposed, what was accepted, what geometry was explored, and where structural invalidity or degeneracy arose.

This benchmark-first stance matters methodologically. It provides a grounded basis for evaluating whether current notions such as loads, `temp_force`, reactions, and acceptance policies are sufficient for equilibrium-informed growth, or whether more explicit external equilibrium scaffolds and support-seeking mechanisms must be introduced. It also allows material efficiency, residual behavior, and intermediate-state admissibility to be assessed before broader ambitions such as large-scale search, latent-space organization, or reinforcement learning are introduced.

Diversity and typological variation remain major research aims, but they are treated as downstream objectives after benchmark grounding. Once the benchmark contract is credible, the same layered machinery may be extended to compare multiple valid solutions for a shared problem and to investigate a wider family of static schemes, including trusses, branching compression systems, tensegrities, cable nets, gridshells, and shells. Construction-aware ambitions also remain important, particularly staged stability, scaffold-free growth, and geometric conformance to curved surfaces, but these are later extensions rather than claims about the current validated state.

Taken together, the project defines a technically constrained path toward a structural generation engine in which structural form emerges through sequential growth guided by equilibrium-related signals, benchmark evidence, and explicit interpretability. The contribution is therefore not only a grammar or a viewer, but a layered experimental framework for testing when structural emergence is admissible, materially efficient, and eventually extensible to broader typologies and learned guidance.
